About the Workshop

Context and Purpose

Digital evidence now sits at the centre of how we investigate crime, respond to cyber incidents, and settle disputes over what was actually said, shown, or shared. Messaging apps, social platforms, and synthetic media have multiplied the volume of that evidence while making its origin and integrity harder to establish. Forensic readiness, the capacity to collect, preserve, and rely on digital evidence before an incident rather than scrambling after one, has become a precondition for trust in social communications.

This one-day workshop, hosted by the UQ Cyber Research Centre at The University of Queensland, brings investigators, practitioners, clinicians, and researchers together to work through where forensic practice needs to go next. The forenoon track examines the problems law enforcement faces with volatile, encrypted, and manipulated evidence. The afternoon track turns to healthcare, where forensic readiness is part of keeping clinical systems running and patient data protected through and after a cyber incident.

Each track pairs keynote addresses from industry and academic leaders with live demonstrations of student research projects from our centre, so that open problems are discussed alongside working tools and prototypes.

Workshop Structure

Two Tracks

The day is split into a forenoon and an afternoon track. Each opens with keynote addresses and closes with live demonstrations of student research projects.

Track 1 of 2
Forensics Challenges for Law Enforcement
ForenoonKeynotesStudent Demos

Investigators now handle evidence that is volatile, spread across cloud services and endpoints, frequently encrypted, and increasingly fabricated. This track examines live response and endpoint visibility at scale, acquisition of evidence from devices and social platforms, authentication of images, audio, and video presented as evidence, and the standards that keep digital findings admissible.

Keynotes: Mike Cohen, Prof. Parag Rughani
Demos: Jack, Noah, Minkyung, Aditya, Anurag

Track 2 of 2
Forensics to Build Cyber Resilience for Health Care
AfternoonKeynotesStudent Demos

Hospitals and health providers run on connected clinical systems, medical devices, and shared patient records, and an incident in any of them is a patient safety issue as much as a data breach. This track treats forensic readiness as a resilience capability: evidence preservation designed into clinical environments, investigation that does not interrupt care, and the integrity of medical data and imaging.

Keynotes: Prof. Rajat Subhra Chakraborty, Matt Walker, Marc Sarossy (to be confirmed)
Demos: Gagandeep, Harshith, Samudi, Gaurav

Workshop Focus

Focus Areas

Three questions run across both tracks and frame the discussion that follows each keynote and demonstration.

Focus 1Readiness by Design

Planning evidence collection, logging, retention, and chain of custody before an incident occurs, so that organisations can answer what happened quickly and defensibly instead of reconstructing it from whatever survived.

Focus 2Trust in Social Communications

Establishing the provenance and authenticity of messages, images, voice, and video shared across social platforms, including detection of manipulated and synthetic media and the evidential weight such content can carry.

Focus 3Resilience and Response

Forensic investigation that supports recovery rather than delaying it, with particular attention to healthcare settings where systems must stay available, patient data must stay protected, and findings must feed back into stronger defences.
